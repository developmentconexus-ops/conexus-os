# Conexus OS decision register

These are the decisions in force. A decision is reopened only by its trigger: material evidence such as a changed requirement, a new real consumer, a newly reachable failure or an external change. Git keeps the history and the reasons behind superseded decisions. The owner guide holds the rule in full.

| ID | Decision | Why | Owner guide | Reopen when |

| C-015 | Better Auth, a library inside the Hub, signs people in and holds their sessions (amended by the operator on 2026-10-09; it replaces Keycloak): accounts, passwords, email verification, and SSO per company through its OIDC and SAML plugin when a company needs it. Conexus owns Workspaces, membership, invitations, identity mapping, and Workspace and Project grants. Provider roles, groups, claims and organizations grant nothing. Decided, not built. | Conexus stays sovereign over who may do what, with one service less and identity data in its own database ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md)). One owner for membership, not a second roster in the provider. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md), [D](../reference/database.md) | Better Auth proves unfit, a stable identity cannot be kept, or a company needs SCIM or an identity feature Better Auth lacks. |
| C-020 | The Builder path is a Project, a Mastra Thread, Project working state, a minimal `BuilderRun` and a checked Preview. Mastra owns the harness. Conexus owns authorization, source, settlement and artifact identity. There is no second conversation store. A failed candidate may leave Preview unavailable and stays repairable. | One owner per concept, and no parallel store. | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | Implementation evidence breaks the boundary. |
| C-021 | A Project is one publishable product with several persistent conversations, each answered by one principal agent. Source admission, artifact health and publication are three gates. An enterprise Connection reaches a Project by binding (C-030). The Project policy `SHARED` or `PER_USER`, Brain, Automations and Work are destination, not built. | The product direction the operator set. | [P](../product/contract.md) | The operator changes direction, or a rule cannot hold on native Mastra mechanisms. |
| C-023 | The Builder sandbox (E2B) has open internet egress. The smoke check refuses requests the app makes outside itself. The Hub's `web_fetch` refuses a query string, a `#` part, credentials and long or data-shaped paths. A log records the hosts each sandbox reaches. Planned after the Q5 verdict: only package registries and documentation hosts. | Keeps the Builder simple while its real needs are learned. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | A run fetches or sends something it should not, the needed hosts are known, or a Project holds data that must not leave. |
| C-024 | One installation serves one company, except a validation installation (amended by the operator on 2026-10-09): it may serve several companies, one Workspace each, and everything a company configures (model accounts, the model default, connections, people) belongs to its Workspace. A company that needs isolation runs its own installation. Published is a separate explicit gate. The Workspace scoping is decided, not built. | The cheapest way to let several small companies validate Conexus with one operator, without a tenancy model above the Workspace ([hosting study, tenancy](../research/hosting/tenancy.md)). | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A company needs a level above its Workspace, a disclosure between Workspaces is observed, or one installation must host companies that need isolation from each other. |
| C-026 | The installation administrator is a Conexus IAM role, separate from Workspace roles. Its actions are in guide S. | Installation-wide actions must not belong to a Workspace owner. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Another administrative tier is needed, or an administrator must reach Project content. |
| C-027 | A provider subscription with no sign-in the Hub can call directly is connected through a Hub-hosted sign-in adapter (`apps/hub/src/builder/google-ai-pro/`). Its credential is a Conexus model account like any other, and no provider secret sits in configuration. | A person's own subscription should work without a second credential store. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | The provider changes its sign-in record or starts rotating refresh tokens, or an official sign-in appears. |
| C-028 | Applications run as a managed platform. Project Git is the source of truth for source, handlers, migrations and manifest. Conexus owns application data, app access, Connection binding, Release and Publish. Generated privileged code runs outside the Hub with scoped authority. No permanent backend per Project. | Proves one safe boundary at a time before a standalone factory. | [A](../reference/architecture.md), [P](../product/contract.md) | The generated-code boundary is unsafe, an app needs a long-lived custom server, or direction changes. |
| C-029 | The platform always records every Connector call through Mastra observability, with timings, Project, consumer, operation, status and result. Content is captured only when an installation administrator turns it on for a limited time. Credentials are never recorded. Content capture is not built. | Diagnosis needs a record, and secrets must never leak into it. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Mastra observability cannot hold a needed field, or cost or retention becomes material. |
| C-030 | Each external system has one integrator: a `ConnectorDefinition` and an adapter. Integrations are any external system a company uses (ERP, CRM, spreadsheets, APIs, MCP servers), not only an ERP (amended on 2026-10-09). A Connection is one account, and a company may hold several. The Workspace owner creates it and binds it to a Project under a local name (C-049). One Hub executor sends native requests. Access is one coarse level, read now. The vendor-side principal makes it read-only. No operation catalog and no per-operation grant. Integrated data is read through, not copied, for now. The Workspace scoping is decided, not built. | A stable local name keeps generated apps working, and a transport check cannot make a native query read-only. | [P](../product/contract.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | A vendor gives no read-only principal, a second integrator breaks the pattern, an app must write, or an app needs history an integrated system cannot answer. |
| C-031 | Conexus designs its own screens. `@mastra/playground-ui` supplies parts only. Basic parts are repainted with Conexus tokens. Agent display parts are allowed. Structure blocks (`AppShell`, `MainSidebar`, `ChatShell`, `new/settings`) are frozen. | One pattern everywhere. | [V](../../DESIGN.md) | A screen needs a structure block with no Conexus equivalent. |
| C-032 | The Builder runs on a Conexus harness over Mastra's engine (`AgentController`, `createCodingAgent`), with one `build` mode and a Conexus tool contract. Each Project's source is a Git repository on the Hub, and only the Hub moves `main` (where the Builder runs, and with it Git, is pending: A7 and A6). Model accounts belong to the company: the Workspace owner connects them, sealed, and the Workspace's members use them (amended by the operator on 2026-10-09 with C-024). This includes a provider subscription connected through C-027's adapter. No account is shared outside its Workspace. Model calls run in the Hub, and the sandbox holds no secret. `@mastra/code-sdk` stays a pinned library. Decided, not built. | The prompt, credentials and source custody must be Conexus's own, and what a company configures belongs to it. | [A](../reference/architecture.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | A Mastra upgrade breaks those beta APIs, a company needs a forge for source, a person must keep a private account, or a provider's terms forbid a subscription's use by a company. |
| C-033 | New apps use the REACT_VITE_V2 stack, pinned in `apps/hub/compiler-template/package.json`. The Builder cannot install a package. Screens call the server through a client generated from `conexus/manifest.json`. One Hub-owned check admits a revision: `generate`, `typecheck`, `build`, `server`, `boot`. A type error blocks. A failed `boot` is reported and never blocks. | The product is dashboards and forms, and the check must not be the candidate's own file. | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A needed component is missing, models fail repeatedly on the stack, or the check is too slow. |
| C-036 | Conversations private to the person who started them: decided, not built. Today there is one Mastra resource per Project, so every Project member sees every conversation. | Privacy by default for each person's work. | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | Two people must share one conversation, or a member must see another's. |
| C-037 | A Project's handler can read the schema, table and column names of the other Projects of its own company, inside its company's database, never their rows and never another company's (a database per company, C-043). Accepted by the operator on 2026-10-02 for the pilot and on 2026-10-09 for the validation installation. The database per company is decided, not built. | A database per Project costs about 7 MB and seconds to create; a schema per Project inside the company's database costs almost nothing ([database study](../research/database/study.md), S9). | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | A name carries data, or a company's apps must hide their names from each other. |
| C-038 | A deleted Project's prompts and source stay in trace spans for up to 30 days. Accepted for the pilot by the operator on 2026-10-02. Replaced once C-045's metadata-only spans are built. | Traces keep their own retention. | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | An erasure request, or a trace store with another retention. |
| C-039 | S1, spec 0015, keeps its child specs, one per part (`0015-part-<owner>.md`), each approved and built separately. This is the operator's exception of 2026-10-06 to guide L, which arrived after S1 began. Every new wave follows the current wave flow. | S1 had already begun under the earlier method; changing its approved parts mid-build would duplicate the design. Parts 4, 5 and 6 keep their child-spec approvals. | [L](../development/delivery.md#waves) | Another wave asks for child specs with a build. |
| C-040 | This repository is the single repository for Conexus OS. Its `docs/development/` guides own engineering and repository method; the Conexus stage skills apply that method. Workstreams and units are issues here, and the roadmap owns gates, waves, work in progress and the next action. | One owner for method and status prevents competing copies. Private operating details stay outside the public repository under guide S §7. | [L](../development/delivery.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md#7-data-protection-and-egress) | A second product repository needs the method and copying it would create two owners. |
| C-041 | Work runs two ways. Small, well-defined issues go to the Factory, which also reviews every pull request. Product development happens with the operator through the planning session and the Conexus wave stages. Exploration uses `spike/` branches that never merge; a run counts as gate proof only when declared so before it runs. Qualification waves receive independent `interrogate` review. The operator may dismiss a Factory finding that is not a leak or security gap under guide L. | The Factory handles bounded work while the planning session brings product choices to the operator. The stage skills replace the earlier combined product workflow. | [L](../development/delivery.md) | A merged defect would have been caught by independent review, or the Factory's reviews stop catching real problems. |
| C-042 | One VPS in São Paulo (Hostinger KVM 2, a month on trial, then 12 months) runs Conexus with Docker Compose from one image, with the modes `hub` and `migrate`. A separate Builder process (A7) and the data service (app runtime decision 3) are pending. Cloudflare is in front: DNS, TLS at the edge and a Tunnel, so the VPS opens no port. Secrets are files (`*_FILE`), placed by the deploy from one named source of truth, still to be chosen. GitHub Actions builds the image into GHCR. Errors and logs go to the Sentry and Grafana Cloud free tiers, and mail to Resend. Decided, not built. | The VPS's only irreplaceable contents are the sealing key and, until A6, Conexus Git. The key is escrowed off the VPS (C-043), and Git is copied to R2. So price decides: the VPS is the cheapest São Paulo machine with root and Docker, and the image moves to any VM or to Cloudflare Containers ([deployment study](../research/deployment/study.md) §9, §10). | [A](../reference/architecture.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | The VPS falls short in CPU or uptime, its price changes materially, or the stateless refactor makes Cloudflare Containers cheaper with a São Paulo placement. |
| C-043 | PostgreSQL 17 on Neon in São Paulo. A control project holds the databases `hub` and `builder`. Each company gets its own Neon project, created by the operator with the company, and one database inside it (confirmed by the operator on 2026-10-09, knowing it costs more on a paid plan at scale). That database has one schema and one login per app and environment (`p_<project>_preview`, `p_<project>_published`). Conexus is never a superuser: the provider's admin role runs only migrations, the provisioner and the backup of `hub` (it bypasses row security on Neon; where an admin does not, a backup role reads through a policy). Start on the Free plan. A greenfield baseline replaces the migration chain before the first company's data, and nothing is kept for compatibility. **Backups:** Neon's point in time, plus nightly dumps encrypted with the operator's public key and sent to R2, in a bucket with an object lock (the VPS's token cannot delete them) and a lifecycle rule (erasure has a bound). The sealing key is escrowed off the VPS, encrypted to the operator. Decided, not built. | The layout runs without superuser on PostgreSQL 17 (deployment spikes P0 to P4, the provider probe). A project per company restores and deletes one company alone and gives each its own free quota. Dump and restore (P3) keeps the provider replaceable. | [D](../reference/database.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Neon's limits or prices change materially, a measured latency hurts, a company needs its data on its own server, or paid compute for company projects exceeds what grouping companies in shared projects would cost (on Launch about R$490 against R$360 at four companies, and R$5,700 against R$2,700 at 100, in `cost.mjs`). |
| C-044 | Tenant isolation has two walls. The first is typed admission in the Hub. The second is one row-level security policy per company-scoped table: `workspace_id = nullif(current_setting('app.workspace_id', true), '')::uuid`, with `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`, a runtime role that owns no table and is never the provider's admin. The Workspace is set only in `platform/db.ts`, with `SET LOCAL` (`is_local = true`), once per transaction, from the admission proof. A check refuses any session-level set, and a test proves a reused connection after another company's transaction, and after an aborted one, reads nothing. The baseline lists every table that is not company-scoped with what protects it (Better Auth's accounts and sessions, the operator, scheduling, the migration ledger, allocations). It also defines how admission resolves a Project's Workspace and which role cross-company jobs use. It replaces the earlier machinery of many policies and a role switch per transaction. Decided, not built. | A missed filter must still return no other company's row (spikes P1 and P4, the provider probe). `nullif` is needed because a reused connection reads `''`. A session-level set would carry one company into the next transaction. The provider's admin bypasses row security on Neon and Supabase (P4). This reopens spec 0018's removal of row security on its wave. | [D](../reference/database.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | A policy measurably slows a hot query, or a needed query cannot run under it. |
| C-045 | Mastra's store lives in its own database `builder`, never in the Hub's database and never one store per company. Conversations, messages and memory are scoped by the resource id `workspace:<w>:project:<p>`: at `@mastra/core` 1.71.0 those tables carry `resourceId` only. Spans also carry `organizationId` = Workspace and keep metadata only; content is captured only by a time-boxed administrator action (C-029). A company is purged by its resource-id prefix and its `organizationId`. The Builder reaches models through the Hub (C-032). Automations, schedules and company knowledge use Mastra's PostgreSQL storage when that milestone comes, with no Redis. Decided, not built. | Conversations and spans are company data. One store scoped by a key Mastra already queries is native, and a store per company is not ([Builder service study](../research/builder-service/study.md)). Mastra's table schemas were checked on the installed version. | [A](../reference/architecture.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Mastra adds an organization key to memory, or the Builder moves out of the Hub with other store needs. |
| C-046 | Generated app code runs on Cloudflare Workers for Platforms. One dispatch Worker of ours fronts every app, and each app version is one user Worker with its static files. An outbound Worker refuses every network call: `fetch`, `connect()` and WebSockets. Each call has a CPU limit. The user Worker holds no credential: it receives only the `db`, `caller` and `connectors` stubs, and the handler contract stays `handler(input, { db, caller, connectors })`. Workers may keep an isolate between requests, so the check refuses mutable module state, or the reuse is recorded. An isolated-vm executor is the local adapter and the tested way out, never a production path on the machine that holds the secrets. The bubblewrap runner, its relay and certificate login, and app and Preview serving in the Hub are deleted. Re-confirmed by the operator after the VPS choice. Decided, not built. | Generated code is treated like code from the internet (guide S §8). The VPS holds every company's keys, so untrusted code must not run there. Cloudflare supplies the outer sandbox, its patching and a CPU limit per call for $25 a month ([app runtime study](../research/app-runtime/study.md) §9, §12). | [A](../reference/architecture.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Cloudflare's monthly bill exceeds the cost of one more small VPS, or a measured edge latency harms real apps. Then a separate VPS for apps, never the Hub's. |
| C-047 | Conexus depends on standard protocols where they exist: PostgreSQL, the S3 API, an OCI image, OpenTelemetry, OIDC. It writes a port only where none exists, and each port has a production adapter and a local one. There are two such ports: the Builder's sandbox behind Mastra's `WorkspaceSandbox`, and the app runtime, whose port is deploy, route and invoke, with Workers for Platforms and the isolated-vm executor as adapters. A vendor's own API stays in tooling or behind one of those ports. Errors go through OpenTelemetry, or a Sentry adapter that passes the redaction table (guide S §7). There is no general cloud layer of our own. Decided, not built. | Changing a provider must be configuration, not product code, and two adapters prove a seam ([deployment study](../research/deployment/study.md) §11). | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A needed capability exists only behind one vendor's API, or a second adapter cannot be kept working. |
| C-048 | Everything lives on one domain, `conexus.fun` (C-OS-001), with no second domain in the first version. The Hub is at `hub.conexus.fun`, and each app at `<app>--<company>.conexus.fun`: one level deep, so Cloudflare's free certificate covers it. Each address is a unique allocation from the registry; `--` keeps the split unambiguous. A Preview's address follows the same rule (implementation). Apps share the Hub's site, and apps of different companies share it with each other, so three safeguards apply. Hub and app sessions use `__Host-` cookies. The Hub, and the dispatch Worker for apps, check `Origin` and `Sec-Fetch-Site` on every state-changing request. The names `hub`, `api`, `data`, `www`, `mail`, `preview` and every product name are reserved. A company's own domain comes later, through Cloudflare for SaaS. Decided, not built. | The operator chose one domain and no extra cost for the MVP on 2026-10-09. Mitra does the same: its studio is at `agent.mitralab.io` and its apps at `<workspace>-<project>.prod.mitralab.io` and `.build.mitralab.io`, all on one domain ([Mitra study](../research/mitra/full-study.md)). A page of one app is same-site with the Hub and with every other app, so cookies and origin checks carry the separation ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md) §5). | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md), [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A cookie or a cross-site request crosses between an app and the Hub or another app, or a company needs its own domain. A second registrable domain for apps closes it structurally. |
| C-049 | The operator creates each company: its Workspace and its Neon project. The company owner connects its integrations and binds them to Projects (C-030). Whether the operator invites the first owner or creates the user is decided later. Decided, not built. | A validation installation keeps the operator in control of who enters, and integrations are the product's reach beyond one ERP ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md)). | [P](../product/contract.md), [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md) | Companies must sign up on their own, or the owner cannot be trusted with a company's integrations. |
| C-050 | Accepted risk: one VPS and one sealing key serve every company of the validation installation. Reduced at no cost: generated code runs off the VPS (C-046); the VPS opens no port, and SSH goes only through the Tunnel with Cloudflare Access; security updates install unattended; containers run without root on a read-only disk; each company has its own data key, sealed by the master key, so destroying a company's key erases its credentials; app logins use a separate key; the master key is escrowed off the VPS, encrypted to the operator. Residual, accepted by the operator on 2026-10-09: a full takeover of the running VPS reaches every company. Decided, not built. | It is the price of one shared installation (C-024), and the reduction leaves only a takeover of the running machine ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md) §11). | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md), [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A company needs isolation from the others, a takeover or a leaked key is suspected, or the installation grows past the validation companies. |
| C-051 | Accepted risk: the Neon endpoints are reachable from the internet; an IP allowlist needs the Scale plan. Residual, accepted by the operator on 2026-10-09: a leaked password works from anywhere until it is rotated. Decided, not built. | Strong passwords and verified TLS are the protection the Free and Launch plans allow; a database on the VPS would close the endpoint at the cost of running backups ([deployment study](../research/deployment/study.md)). | [S](../reference/security-and-authority.md), [D](../reference/database.md) | A password leaks, the Scale plan is taken, or Neon offers private networking on a cheaper plan. |
| C-052 | Accepted risk: Conexus Git lives on the VPS disk until A6 decides where it lives. Reduced at no cost: an encrypted `git bundle` goes to R2 on each admitted revision. Residual, accepted by the operator on 2026-10-09: work not yet admitted can be lost with the VPS; it also lives in the E2B sandbox and in the conversation. Decided, not built. | A copy per admission bounds the loss to unadmitted work without a forge ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md) §11). | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | A6 moves Git off the VPS, or admitted work is lost. |
| C-053 | Accepted risk: a deploy restarts the Hub and drops the Builder's live turns. Reduced at no cost: a deploy waits until no Builder run is active, and runs at night. Residual, accepted by the operator on 2026-10-09: a process crash still drops a live turn, until the stateless refactor rebuilds Builder sessions; the conversation history stays in Mastra's store. Decided, not built. | A turn in progress lives in the process; a restart loses only it, and the operator controls when deploys happen ([target design](../research/architecture/target-design.md) §11). | [A](../reference/architecture.md) | The stateless refactor is built, or a dropped turn harms a company's work. |
| C-054 | Accepted risk: generated apps depend on Cloudflare Workers for Platforms. Residual, accepted by the operator on 2026-10-09: switching takes days, not months. | C-055 | Accepted risk: company data is processed outside Brazil.
